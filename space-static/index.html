naam-match

Is “आयुष देवांगन” the same person as “Aayush Dewangan”? Transparent Hindi/English name matching for Indian land and loan records. Runs entirely in your browser; nothing you type leaves this page.

Try these (all synthetic)

Paste one pair per line as name A | name B, or load a CSV with two columns. Up to 500 pairs.

Why this exists

Land records in Chhattisgarh and most of Hindi-speaking India are written in Devanagari. Sale deeds, bank files and loan applications are usually in English, and the same person's name is spelt differently in each. A lender's title check, or a property-data platform joining a deed to a land record, breaks right there.

naam-match is a small, dependency-free matcher (Python and JavaScript) that explains every decision it makes: transliterate, clean, drop honorifics, fold spelling variants, ignore word order, then score each word.

The design choice

It would rather send a pair to a human than wrongly join two people's property records. Initials (R. K. Sahu) can only ever lead to Send for review, never to an automatic match.

Read these numbers with care. The data is synthetic and the rules were written by the same person who wrote the generator, so the benchmark is optimistic. Real records add OCR errors, father's or husband's names, clan names used as surnames, and spellings no rule anticipates. It is a heuristic, not a trained model, and not production-ready on its own.
